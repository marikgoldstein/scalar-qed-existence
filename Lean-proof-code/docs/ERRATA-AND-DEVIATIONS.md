# Errata and deviations

This record lists where the Lean statements and proofs differ from the printed source. An *erratum* is a printed assertion that is false; a *deviation* is a deliberate difference (scope, conventions, reformulation, a supplied argument for a step the source leaves implicit, or a different proof route). A missing argument for a true step is listed as a deviation, not an erratum. Source: M. Goldstein, A. Guevara, *Osterwalder–Schrader axioms for scalar QED₃ in the Higgs phase*, hexagon:2611.00321v1 ([GG-OS]); locations are its theorem and equation numbers. Entries are counted once each. ## [GG-OS]

### Errata

None.

### Deviations

1. **Section 2, Remark 2.1 (coefficients).** The theorems hold for every coefficient record of Remark 2.1 (γ, λ₀, ρ₀,₀ > 0, κ ≥ 0, δm_B real; `GGOS.Coeffs`), not only for the level-N values of Section 2. Lattice quantities are in lattice units: a torus has `n` sites per side with `n` even and positive (`GGOS.Side`), the physical side being L = n ε_N.
2. **Section 2 (lattice definitions).** The lattice definitions (sites, bonds, plaquettes, the coboundaries, closed forms, the curvature, lattice symmetries and their action) are stated over any commutative ring; the statements use only ℤ (the fine lattice) and `ZMod n` (the torus).
3. **Theorem 3.1.** Split into two theorems: the first sentence (`GGOS.gaussian_domination`, which also states that the torus state is a probability measure and that the exponential is integrable) and the second sentence (`GGOS.gaussian_moments`, with the integrability of each moment stated). Finiteness that the source asserts implicitly is stated as explicit integrability clauses, since a Lean integral of a non-integrable function is 0.
4. **Theorem 3.1, proof.** The mass at zero source is bounded directly by Gaussian estimates instead of the Poisson–Bessel representation; the nonnegativity of the Bessel coefficients I_q(z) is replaced by the nonnegativity of the Fourier coefficients of e^{z cos φ}, proved by induction on powers of cos φ; the case A = Σ_p a_p² = 0 of the moment bound, which the printed optimisation divides by, is proved separately.
5. **Definition 4.1 and the text after it.** Stated: both level families are bounded, ‖σ‖ is a seminorm, and it is admissible. Not stated: the explicit compact-support bounds and the Riemann-sum limits as N → ∞. The finiteness bound is proved by an integer-box count instead of the volume estimate.
6. **Proposition 4.2.** `GGOS.torus_estimate` ([TorusEstimate](../GGOS/Frozen/TorusEstimate.lean)) is the torus inequality; integrability of Φ_N(σ)² under the torus state is stated explicitly; the infinite-volume clause for limit-point states is proved as a lemma for Theorem 7.1 (`GGOS.Work.trunc_le_pairNorm_of_isLimitPoint`). "The support of σ embeds in the torus" is made precise as: the closed plaquette squares and sites that enter Φ_N(σ) lie in an open cube of side L (`GGOS.Embeds`); the injectivity of the quotient map on these cells, asserted in the source, is proved.
7. **Lemma 5.1.** `GGOS.torus_symmetry` is the torus claim of the proof (invariance of every torus state under the group generated by lattice translations, reflections in site planes and cubic rotations, for bounded measurable observables of the curvature and density variables). The proof supplies the orientation signs, the bijection of closed integer forms and the change of variables on the half-open bond interval. The infinite-volume statement is used in Proposition 6.1 as a premise; for limit points it is proved as a lemma for Theorem 7.1 (`GGOS.Work.latticeInvariant_of_isLimitPoint`).
8. **Proposition 6.1.** The estimate premise is the truncated form of Remark 6.2 (i), ω_N(min(|Φ_N(σ)|, 2)) ≤ p(σ) with coefficient 1, for an admissible seminorm p ([PassageToLimit](../GGOS/Frozen/PassageToLimit.lean)). Because ι_N is the realisation of the pinned scheme (`GGOS.iota`), the bound (2.4), |ι_N(a)| ≤ M(a), holds by construction and is not a premise; the level-dependent ρ₀,₀ enters as a parameter `ρ0 : ℕ → ℝ` with `0 < ρ0 N`, which fixes Ω_N in the state property.
9. **Proposition 6.1, proof.** The invariance under translations, and under every motion with linear part in the cubic group, is obtained by approximating each displacement on the meshes ε_{N_m}ℤ³ and using admissibility (the cubic group about a point of a mesh is the subgroup that fixes it), the separability of each fixed-support test space is obtained by embedding it through its derivatives into a countable product of spaces of continuous maps, and the diagonal extraction over the resulting countable dense set is replaced by the sequential compactness of a product of closed unit discs. Three premises (ρ₀,₀ > 0, the separate discrete time invariance, which follows from lattice invariance, and the seminorm property of p) are not needed by the proof.
10. **Definition 2.3.** `GGOS.IsLimitPoint`: there are sides L_n → ∞ and a Borel probability measure μ_N on Ω_N such that the torus expectations of every bounded continuous local function converge to its μ_N-integral (equivalent to weak convergence of the finite-dimensional laws), and ω_N(F) = ∫ F dμ_N for every bounded Borel local F. General sides are allowed (the dyadic choice of the source is not needed for Theorem 7.1).
11. **Theorem 7.1, proof.** The premises of Proposition 6.1 are proved for limit points: the state property, the lattice invariance (from the torus symmetry by weak convergence and the uniqueness of measures with equal finite-dimensional marginals), the discrete time invariance, and the truncated estimate (by min(|X + Y|, 2) ≤ min(|X|, 2) + |Y| for the curvature part X and the density part Y, bounding min(|X|, 2) on large tori and passing this bounded continuous local observable to μ_N by weak convergence, instead of passing the unbounded X² to μ_N as in the source), with p the seminorm of Definition 4.1 ([LimitFunctional](../GGOS/Frozen/LimitFunctional.lean), lemmas in [GGOS/Work](../GGOS/Work/)).
